# Establish value

Start with the current request, not a proxy metric or old instruction. Identify who is trying to do what, the decision or outcome that matters, the constraints and serious risks, and what observable evidence would distinguish success from failure. Separate the user's stated purpose and direct evidence from inferred needs; preserve uncertainty and counterevidence. Do not assume the buyer, user, and decision-maker are the same.

Proportion the work to the task: use existing context (the conversation, project instructions, specs, issues, the code itself) and avoid turning a small change into an intake ceremony. If a consequential uncertainty cannot be resolved from available evidence, expose it and seek the smallest useful observation or decision, such as one focused question rather than a questionnaire. Treat tests, model judgments, and simulated users (including agents role-playing users) as evidence of only what they actually establish, not proof of real-world value.

Report briefly: the outcome in one or two sentences, the success evidence, key constraints and risks, and open unknowns.

Use this independently or hand the outcome and unknowns to kaizen-observe-flow. If the goal shifts, revisit the outcome rather than quietly changing it.
